Scoring & Stats Settings

How player rankings are calculated in Sideline, and what every admin setting does

Reference guide · generated 21 July 2026

Every player carries a rating from 0–100. It blends two independent signals — what teammates think of them (peer votes) and what they actually did on the pitch (approved stats) — and then dampens the result based on how much data we have, so a player with two votes can never leapfrog someone with two seasons of evidence.

1. The 0–10 rating scale

When voting after a match, players rate each teammate from 0 to 10. Each number has a shared descriptor so everyone interprets the scale the same way. 5 is average — an ordinary, solid performance, not a bad one.

RatingDescriptorTier
0Didn’t turn upLow
1Very poorLow
2PoorLow
3Below averageLow
4FairMid
5AverageMid
6DecentHigh
7GoodHigh
8Very goodHigh
9ExcellentElite
10World classElite

These 0–10 votes are later mapped linearly onto the 0–100 scale (a raw vote average of 7.0 → 70).

2. The big picture

A player’s final score is a weighted blend of two adjusted sub-scores:

final = peerAdjusted × peerWeight + statsAdjusted × statsWeight
  • Peer weight and stats weight always add up to 100%. The default is Peer 60% / Stats 40%.
  • If a player has no approved stats, stats weight becomes 0 and the final score is 100% peer-based.
  • Both sub-scores are damped toward a neutral score (default 50) when we have little data, so new or thinly-rated players sit near the middle instead of at the extremes.

Why “damping toward neutral”?

A single 9/10 vote shouldn’t make someone the best player in the club. Instead of trusting a raw average blindly, we scale it by a confidence factor between 0 and 1. Low confidence pulls the score back toward 50; full confidence lets the raw score through unchanged.

adjusted = neutral + confidence × (raw − neutral)

3. Confidence tiers

Every player is labelled with a tier that reflects how trustworthy their score is:

Tiers are informational — they don’t change the maths, but they tell you how much to trust a number.

4. Peer score, step by step

The peer score turns raw teammate votes into a damped 0–100 value. Five stages:

Step 1 — Collect valid votes per match

Only votes flagged as in-scope / included in calculation are counted. Votes an admin has removed or marked out-of-scope are ignored.

Step 2 — Remove outliers (per match)

Within a single match, we drop rogue votes using the interquartile-range (IQR) rule — the same technique used to ignore one friend’s spite-vote or one buddy’s inflated 10.

  • Fewer than 4 votes: keep them all (too few to judge outliers).
  • Otherwise compute Q1 and Q3, and keep only votes within [Q1 − 1.5·IQR, Q3 + 1.5·IQR].
  • If filtering leaves fewer than 3 votes, fall back to the median of the original votes.

Step 3 — Aggregate each match into one number

The surviving votes for a match are collapsed to a single value. By default this is the mean; admins can switch to median or trimmed mean (see Advanced fairness).

Step 4 — Combine matches (recency-weighted)

A player’s per-match values are averaged with the three most recent matches counting double, so current form matters more than old results. The result (0–10) is multiplied by 10 to land on 0–100.

peerRaw(0–100) = weightedAverage(perMatchValues) × 10

Step 5 — Damp by confidence

Two confidence factors are multiplied together, then applied:

  • Vote confidence — min(1, validVotes / peerFullConfidenceVotes) (default full at 12 votes).
  • Match confidence — min(1, matchesPlayed / matchCountFullConfidence) (default full at 3 matches).
peerAdjusted = 50 + (voteConf × matchConf) × (peerRaw − 50)

Example — a promising newcomer

test_user has played 2 matches and received 6 valid votes averaging 8.0/10.

  • peerRaw = 8.0 × 10 = 80
  • voteConf = min(1, 6 / 12) = 0.5
  • matchConf = min(1, 2 / 3) = 0.667
  • combined confidence = 0.5 × 0.667 = 0.333
  • peerAdjusted = 50 + 0.333 × (80 − 50) = 50 + 10 = 60.0

Even with glowing 8s, her thin sample keeps her at a modest 60 — she’ll climb as more data arrives.

5. Stats score, step by step

The stats score rewards on-pitch output — goals, assists and penalties — weighted by playing position. It only uses approved stats from matches where stats scoring is enabled.

Step 1 — Score each match with position multipliers

Each stat is multiplied by a position-specific factor (inspired by Fantasy Premier League). A goal from a goalkeeper is worth more than a goal from a forward, because it’s rarer and more valuable.

PositionGoalAssistPenalty scoredPenalty conceded
GK (goalkeeper)2.01.01.5−1.0
DEF (defender)1.51.01.3−1.3
MID (midfielder)1.251.01.2−1.0
FWD (forward)1.01.21.0−0.8
matchStat = goals·Mgoal + assists·Massist + pensScored·Mpen+ + pensConceded·Mpen−

Step 2 — Average matches (recency-weighted)

As with peer votes, the three most recent matches count double.

Step 3 — Normalise across the squad

Raw stat totals are stretched onto a 0–100 scale relative to the rest of the club: the lowest becomes 0, the highest becomes 100, everyone else sits in between. (If everyone is tied, an absolute anchor is used: 0 → 50, 5 → 75, 10 → 100.)

Step 4 — Damp by confidence

Same idea as peer: min(1, statMatches / statFullConfidenceMatches) (default full at 4 matches), multiplied by match confidence, then applied around neutral.

Example — a clinical midfielder

test_user#2 (MID) across 4 stat-tracked matches averaged 2 goals + 1 assist per game.

  • matchStat = 2 × 1.25 + 1 × 1.0 = 3.5 per match
  • Suppose that normalises to 85 against the squad’s spread.
  • statConf = min(1, 4 / 4) = 1.0, matchConf = min(1, 4 / 3) = 1.0
  • statsAdjusted = 50 + 1.0 × (85 − 50) = 85.0

6. Worked end-to-end example

Putting peer + stats together (default 60/40 blend)

Take a rated player with:

  • peerAdjusted = 72
  • statsAdjusted = 85
final = 72 × 0.60 + 85 × 0.40 = 43.2 + 34.0 = 77.2

If that same player had no approved stats, stats weight would drop to 0 and their final score would simply be their peer score: 72.0.

8. Advanced fairness settings

These are optional refinements. All default to OFF, which reproduces the classic scoring behaviour exactly. Turn them on to make scores fairer or more robust — but note that enabling any of them changes the numbers by design.

Normalize voter harshness

Re-centres each voter’s ballot so their personal average matches the match average. This cancels out habitually harsh or lenient voters while preserving the match’s overall mean.

Example

In a match averaging 6.5, a chronically stingy voter gives everyone ~1 point low (their own average is 5.5). Normalization adds 6.5 − 5.5 = +1.0 to each of their votes, so their opinions still count but their harshness no longer drags players down.

Weight matches by number of voters

A match rated by 14 people is stronger evidence than one rated by 3. When on, each match’s influence is scaled by how many people voted (on top of the recency double-weight).

Per-match vote aggregate

OptionBehaviour
Mean (default)Simple average of surviving votes.
MedianMiddle value — maximally robust to a couple of extreme votes.
Trimmed meanDrops the top and bottom 10%, then averages the rest — a balance of the two.

Example

Votes [4, 7, 7, 8, 10]: mean = 7.2, median = 7, trimmed mean (drop one each end) = 7.33. Median resists that lone 10 the most.

Bayesian shrinkage

A smoother alternative to the hard vote-threshold ramp. It blends a player’s raw score with a neutral prior worth k “pseudo-votes”, so scores glide toward neutral when data is thin instead of stepping.

shrunk = (validVotes × peerRaw + k × 50) / (validVotes + k)

Example (k = 6)

4 votes averaging raw 90: shrunk = (4×90 + 6×50) / (4+6) = (360+300)/10 = 66. The score is pulled from 90 toward neutral because only 4 votes back it. Prior strength (k) controls how hard: higher k = more shrinkage.

Recency decay for inactivity

Pulls long-absent players back toward neutral so the table reflects current form. Confidence decays with a configurable half-life.

decay = 0.5 ^ (daysSinceLastMatch / halfLifeDays)

Example (half-life = 45 days)

A player who hasn’t played for 90 days: decay = 0.5^(90/45) = 0.5² = 0.25. Their confidence — and therefore how far their score sits from neutral — is quartered until they play again.

9. Per-match: “Include stats in scoring”

Each match has an Include stats in scoring checkbox in its settings. When unticked, that match’s goals/assists are recorded and displayed but excluded from the stats score. Use it for friendlies, lopsided teams, or early sessions where the numbers would distort rankings. Peer votes from the match are unaffected.
